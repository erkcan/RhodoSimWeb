Solves the cavity at six cell sizes from 1.25 h to 0.71 h and fits each quantity to q∞ + C h². Error is the estimated error at your mesh: 1.25 × the distance to q∞ (Roache's safety factor) + two standard errors of q∞ + two widths of the scatter that cut cells add around the h² trend. Self-test 41 checks that this estimate covers the true error. "scatter" in the order column means the scatter hides the trend.

CHECKNo z plane falls exactly on the mid-plane. RhodoSim looks for the field maximum at z = 0, so make z from a multiple of the step.


        

        
RhodoSim reads the nearest grid point using one step for x, y and z, so the z step follows the x, y step. Within about one cell of the rounded walls the interpolated field is less accurate: its component along the wall reaches up to 5 % of the peak at 5 mm cells. The beam plane, between straight walls, is not affected. Cartesian samples come from the r–z solution: Ex = Er·x/r, Ey = Er·y/r, Ez = Ez, zero inside the inner conductor and outside the wall. Values are peak amplitudes scaled to the mid-plane normalisation in Operating mode; RhodoSim applies sin(ωt + φ). Defaults match the grid of your CST example and emax in config.ini.

Passes through the cavity

      
A pass is one transit across the cavity, from entering through the outer conductor (r = R2) to leaving it; its time and phase are taken where the electron comes closest to the axis. Phase is the RF phase at that moment relative to the crest: 0° means the field reverses as the electron passes the centre, so it is pushed forward on the way in and on the way out. That gives the largest gain for a fast electron; with the preset cavity a slow one gains most a little later (about +7° for the 40 keV first pass). Negative means early. Loop is the time from this centre crossing to the next; in synchronism it is a whole number of RF periods (one period is 9.30 ns here). Click a pass to move the timeline there.
